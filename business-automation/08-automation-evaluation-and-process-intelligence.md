# 08 — Automation Evaluation and Process Intelligence

## Learning outcomes

By the end of this module, you should be able to:

- explain the role of **08 — Automation Evaluation and Process Intelligence** within Business Automation;
- distinguish the mechanism from adjacent concepts and identify the main architectural trade-offs;
- identify realistic failure modes, control boundaries and operating constraints;
- apply the concept to a representative production design and define evidence that would validate the choice.

## Purpose

Measure whether automation improves the process rather than simply increasing machine activity.

## Outcome metrics

Track cycle time, successful completion, first-pass quality, rework, exception rate, human touch time, backlog, SLA attainment, critical errors and business outcome.

## Automation rate

Automation rate alone can be misleading. A high rate with costly corrections may be worse than selective automation.

## Straight-through processing

Measure cases completed without intervention alongside correctness and consequence.

## Process intelligence

Use workflow/event data to identify bottlenecks, repeated exceptions, loops, handoff delays and rule candidates.

## Before/after

Compare against the baseline established before automation and account for changes in volume or case mix.

## Economics

Include model/tool cost, infrastructure, human review, support, failure/rework and maintenance.

## Feedback loop

```text
Workflow outcomes
 ↓
Process analysis
 ↓
Improvement hypothesis
 ↓
Evaluation
 ↓
Controlled change
```

## Exercise

Create a scorecard for an automation that reduces manual work but may increase downstream correction.

## Takeaway

> Automation is valuable when the business process improves, not when the percentage of machine-executed steps rises.
