# 08 — Enterprise AI Sales

## Purpose

Enterprise AI sales is the process of turning a technically credible AI capability into a trusted business purchase. Enterprise buyers evaluate more than features: they assess business value, security, integration, governance, procurement, risk, and organisational impact.

## Core principle

> Enterprise customers do not buy AI capability alone. They buy a credible path to measurable business value with acceptable risk.

## 1. The enterprise buying group

An AI sale can involve multiple stakeholders:

- business sponsor;
- end users;
- technical/engineering team;
- security;
- legal/privacy;
- procurement;
- finance;
- executive leadership.

Each stakeholder has a different definition of success.

## 2. Sell the problem and outcome

Weak:

> Our platform uses advanced AI agents.

Stronger:

> The platform reduces a costly workflow, improves a measurable outcome, and provides controlled automation.

AI architecture should support the value proposition rather than become the value proposition.

## 3. Discovery

Enterprise discovery should establish:

- current workflow;
- people involved;
- systems used;
- frequency and volume;
- cost of the current process;
- error/failure rate;
- business impact;
- existing alternatives;
- decision owner;
- security and compliance constraints.

## 4. Proof of Value

For enterprise AI, a focused proof of value is often more persuasive than a generic demo.

```text
Business problem
      ↓
Defined baseline
      ↓
Controlled pilot
      ↓
Measured outcome
      ↓
Commercial decision
```

Define success criteria before the pilot begins.

Examples:

- time saved;
- error reduction;
- increased throughput;
- improved conversion;
- improved margin;
- reduced support workload.

## 5. Pilot design

A good pilot should have:

- a narrow scope;
- representative data;
- named stakeholders;
- measurable success criteria;
- defined duration;
- clear security boundaries;
- a route to production if successful.

Avoid pilots that become indefinite free consulting projects.

## 6. Enterprise security review

Expect questions about:

- data handling;
- encryption;
- identity and access;
- tenant isolation;
- logging;
- retention;
- subprocessors;
- model providers;
- incident response;
- business continuity.

A mature sales process has evidence ready rather than treating security as an afterthought.

## 7. Procurement and legal

Enterprise procurement may require:

- contracts;
- data-processing terms;
- service levels;
- security documentation;
- insurance;
- support commitments;
- pricing and payment terms.

Commercial readiness therefore includes operational and legal readiness, not only product readiness.

## 8. Handling AI objections

### “We cannot trust AI.”

Address this with:

- evaluation evidence;
- human oversight;
- auditability;
- controlled permissions;
- clear limitations.

### “We already use a general AI tool.”

Differentiate on workflow integration, domain context, governance, measurable outcomes, and operational execution.

### “We are worried about data.”

Explain the actual data flow, controls, retention, isolation, and provider arrangements.

### “The pilot worked, but we are not ready to deploy.”

Identify the specific production blocker and create a documented path to resolve it.

## 9. Land and expand

A practical enterprise strategy is:

```text
One painful workflow
        ↓
Proof of value
        ↓
Production deployment
        ↓
Additional teams / workflows
        ↓
Enterprise expansion
```

Expansion should follow demonstrated value, not forced feature bundling.

## 10. Enterprise AI sales metrics

Track:

- qualified opportunities;
- pilot conversion;
- proof-of-value success rate;
- sales cycle length;
- average contract value;
- time to production;
- expansion revenue;
- churn;
- implementation effort;
- gross margin by customer.

## 11. SEMLIS application

For SEMLIS, the enterprise sale should ultimately demonstrate a concrete commerce outcome rather than lead with model sophistication.

A potential structure:

```text
Merchant problem
      ↓
Baseline marketplace performance
      ↓
SEMLIS intelligence workflow
      ↓
Recommendation / controlled automation
      ↓
Measured improvement
      ↓
Commercial expansion
```

The strongest proof will come from real customer outcomes: better decisions, reduced wasted effort, improved profitability, or other measurable improvements.

## 12. Enterprise readiness checklist

Before pursuing large enterprise deployments, verify:

1. Is the target problem expensive enough to justify purchase?
2. Is there a measurable baseline?
3. Is the economic value clear?
4. Can the product pass security review?
5. Are permissions and audit controls mature?
6. Can implementation be repeated without excessive custom work?
7. Are contracts and support processes ready?
8. Can the platform scale economically?
9. Is there a clear expansion path?
10. Can the customer explain internally why buying is safer and better than maintaining the current process?

## Takeaway

> Enterprise AI sales is the disciplined conversion of technical capability into trusted, measurable business value.

The strongest sales motion is evidence-led: understand the problem, prove the outcome, remove risk, then scale the relationship.
