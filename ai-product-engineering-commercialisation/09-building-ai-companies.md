# 09 — Building AI Companies

## Purpose

Building an AI company requires more than model access or strong engineering. The company must repeatedly convert customer problems into valuable, reliable products while controlling technical, financial, regulatory, and organisational risk.

## Core principle

> Build a company that compounds learning, distribution, proprietary data, product capability, and customer trust—not merely a model wrapper.

## 1. The AI company system

A durable AI company combines:

```text
Customer Problems
      ↓
Product
      ↓
AI / Software Capability
      ↓
Customer Outcomes
      ↓
Data + Feedback
      ↓
Better Product
      ↓
Retention / Expansion
```

The feedback loop is more strategically important than any individual model.

## 2. Founder-market fit

Strong founders understand the customer problem deeply enough to distinguish:

- real pain from fashionable problems;
- useful automation from unnecessary automation;
- measurable value from impressive demos.

Technical knowledge and domain knowledge can reinforce each other, but neither replaces customer evidence.

## 3. Team design

Early AI companies usually need capabilities across:

- product;
- software engineering;
- AI/ML engineering;
- infrastructure/security;
- design/user experience;
- sales/customer success.

One person may cover several roles initially. The important requirement is that critical capabilities have clear ownership.

## 4. Build vs buy

Use external providers where they create leverage:

- foundation models;
- commodity infrastructure;
- payments;
- authentication;
- routine SaaS tooling.

Build proprietary capability where it creates defensibility:

- domain workflows;
- proprietary data pipelines;
- unique decision logic;
- customer feedback loops;
- integrations that create switching costs;
- specialised evaluation and intelligence.

## 5. Defensibility

Model access alone is rarely sufficient defensibility because competitors may access similar models.

Potential compounding advantages include:

- proprietary data generated through real usage;
- workflow integration;
- distribution;
- customer relationships;
- domain expertise;
- evaluation datasets;
- operational feedback loops;
- trust and compliance maturity.

## 6. Go-to-market

A practical progression is:

```text
Narrow customer segment
      ↓
Painful workflow
      ↓
Proof of value
      ↓
Repeatable deployment
      ↓
Reference customers
      ↓
Repeatable sales
      ↓
Expansion
```

Do not assume that product-market fit and distribution automatically arrive together.

## 7. Funding strategy

Funding should accelerate a validated business rather than substitute for validation.

Capital may fund:

- engineering;
- compute;
- security;
- product development;
- customer acquisition;
- enterprise readiness;
- regulatory work;
- international expansion.

Raise enough to reach the next meaningful value milestone while maintaining disciplined capital allocation.

## 8. AI-specific capital discipline

AI companies should model:

- inference cost;
- infrastructure commitments;
- engineering headcount;
- customer acquisition cost;
- implementation cost;
- gross margin;
- cash runway.

Avoid building infrastructure simply because it is technically interesting.

## 9. Organisational scaling

As the company grows, move from founder intuition toward explicit systems:

- product metrics;
- engineering standards;
- security controls;
- AI evaluation;
- incident management;
- financial reporting;
- hiring processes;
- decision ownership.

The company itself becomes an operating system.

## 10. AI company failure modes

### Building before validating

Creates technical debt around an unproven problem.

### Model worship

Treating model capability as the product's moat.

### Poor unit economics

Growing usage faster than profitable value delivery.

### Excessive customisation

Turning every customer into a separate engineering project.

### Weak distribution

Assuming superior technology automatically creates demand.

### Safety debt

Adding security, governance, and permissions only after deployment.

## 11. SEMLIS application

The long-term SEMLIS company should compound around commerce intelligence rather than simply around access to third-party models.

Potential compounding loop:

```text
Marketplace integrations
        ↓
Commerce data
        ↓
AI insights
        ↓
Customer decisions
        ↓
Observed outcomes
        ↓
Feedback / evaluation data
        ↓
Better intelligence
```

This creates an opportunity for a model-independent intelligence layer where models can change while the domain system, evaluation, workflows, and data advantage continue to compound.

## 12. Founder decision framework

Before pursuing a major initiative, ask:

1. Does it solve a painful customer problem?
2. Does it improve measurable customer value?
3. Does it create a repeatable capability?
4. Does it strengthen the data or feedback loop?
5. Does it improve distribution or retention?
6. Is the unit economics model sustainable?
7. Does it increase or reduce operational risk?
8. Can the capability survive a change of model provider?

## Takeaway

> The strongest AI companies build compounding systems around customers, data, workflows, distribution, and trust.

The model is an important component. It is not the entire company.
