# 02 — Identifying Valuable AI Problems

## Core Principle

Successful AI products start with valuable problems, not available technology.

The correct sequence:

```
Customer Pain

↓

Business Value

↓

AI Opportunity

↓

Product Solution
```

## AI Opportunity Assessment

Strong AI opportunities usually contain:

- repetitive work;
- high manual effort;
- large amounts of data;
- measurable outcomes;
- expensive mistakes;
- clear willingness to pay.

## Avoiding AI Gimmicks

Weak approach:

```
New AI capability

↓

Find a use case
```

Strong approach:

```
Painful business problem

↓

Determine if AI creates a significant advantage
```

## Problem Scoring Framework

Evaluate opportunities across:

- customer pain severity;
- frequency of the problem;
- economic value;
- data availability;
- AI feasibility;
- competitive advantage;
- distribution potential.

## AI Product Categories

### Automation AI

Replacing repetitive workflows.

Examples:

- document processing;
- customer support;
- operational tasks.

### Decision Intelligence AI

Helping humans make better decisions.

Examples:

- forecasting;
- optimisation;
- recommendations.

### Autonomous Agent Systems

AI that can execute controlled workflows.

Examples:

- business assistants;
- research agents;
- operational agents.

## SEMLIS Example

A weak idea:

```
AI chatbot for marketplace sellers
```

A stronger problem:

```
Marketplace sellers lose profit because they cannot analyse thousands of products, fees, ads, stock and customer signals effectively.
```

AI solution:

```
Commerce Intelligence Platform

+

Decision Support

+

Controlled Automation
```

## Final Principle

The strongest AI companies do not win because they have AI.

They win because they solve important problems better than existing alternatives.
