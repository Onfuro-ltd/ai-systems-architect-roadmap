# AI Governance Frameworks

## Core Principle

AI systems need clear ownership, policies, accountability, and lifecycle management.

Capability alone is not enough. Enterprise AI requires governance.

## Governance Architecture

```
AI Strategy
    ↓
Ownership & Accountability
    ↓
Policies & Controls
    ↓
Development Standards
    ↓
Deployment Review
    ↓
Monitoring & Audit
```

## Key Governance Areas

### Ownership

Every AI system should have:

- business owner;
- technical owner;
- risk owner;
- clear escalation paths.

### AI Lifecycle Management

Governance should exist across:

- design;
- development;
- testing;
- deployment;
- monitoring;
- retirement.

### Documentation

Enterprise AI systems should maintain:

- purpose;
- data sources;
- model information;
- permissions;
- evaluation results;
- known limitations.

## Why This Matters

Without governance, organisations cannot reliably answer:

- Who owns this AI decision?
- What data did the system use?
- Why did it produce this result?
- Who approved deployment?

## SEMLIS Relevance

A future commerce AI platform requires governance because agents may influence:

- marketplace operations;
- pricing decisions;
- customer communication;
- financial workflows.

The correct pattern:

```
AI Capability
      ↓
Governance Rules
      ↓
Validation
      ↓
Controlled Operation
      ↓
Audit History
```
