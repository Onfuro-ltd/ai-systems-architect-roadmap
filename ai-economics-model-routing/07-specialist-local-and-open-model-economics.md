# 07 — Specialist, Local and Open-Model Economics

## Learning outcomes

By the end of this module, you should be able to:

- explain the role of **07 — Specialist, Local and Open-Model Economics** within AI Economics and Model Routing;
- distinguish the mechanism from adjacent concepts and identify the main architectural trade-offs;
- identify realistic failure modes, control boundaries and operating constraints;
- apply the concept to a representative production design and define evidence that would validate the choice.

## Purpose

Compare frontier APIs with specialist, open-weight and self-hosted models on complete lifecycle economics.

## Specialist models

A smaller model can outperform a general model on a bounded task after prompting, retrieval or tuning. Evaluate quality before assuming parameter count predicts value.

## Self-hosted economics

Include accelerator lifecycle, utilization, power, facilities, networking, storage, engineering, redundancy, model serving, upgrades and downtime.

## Hosted economics

Include token/media/tool usage, commitments, rate limits, network, provider dependency and operational simplicity.

## Break-even

Break-even depends on workload volume and shape, required quality, utilization, hardware lifetime and staffing—not a universal token count.

## Privacy value

Private/local execution can have strategic or compliance value beyond raw inference price. Quantify constraints separately from cost optimization.

## Hybrid

Stable high-volume workloads may suit specialists/private serving while irregular frontier reasoning stays hosted.

## Exercise

Compare a hosted frontier model, hosted small model and owned specialist model for a high-volume classification workflow.

## Takeaway

> Self-hosting is an economic architecture decision, not a statement of independence.
