# Code of Conduct

## Our standard

AI Systems Architect Roadmap is intended to support rigorous, evidence-driven technical learning and collaboration.

Participants should:

- communicate respectfully and professionally;
- critique claims, experiments, architectures and evidence rather than people;
- distinguish fact, inference and opinion;
- welcome corrections and competing technical perspectives;
- disclose relevant limitations and conflicts where they materially affect a claim;
- avoid hype, harassment and misinformation;
- protect private, confidential and security-sensitive information.

## Unacceptable behaviour

Unacceptable behaviour includes:

- harassment, threats, intimidation or discriminatory abuse;
- personal attacks or sustained hostile conduct;
- publishing another person's private information without permission;
- deliberately submitting secrets, customer data or proprietary material;
- spam, coordinated disruption or deceptive promotion;
- fabricating research, benchmarks, citations or production evidence;
- knowingly misrepresenting vendor marketing as independent evidence.

## Technical disagreement

Strong disagreement is welcome.

A useful technical challenge explains:

1. which claim or design is disputed;
2. what evidence supports the challenge;
3. what alternative explanation or architecture is proposed;
4. what experiment or source could resolve the disagreement.

The goal is not consensus at any cost. The goal is better engineering judgement.

## Scope

This code applies to repository issues, pull requests, reviews and other project spaces, as well as interactions where someone is representing the project.

## Reporting conduct concerns

For non-sensitive problems, use GitHub's normal reporting/moderation controls or raise the issue with maintainers.

For sensitive conduct concerns, use a private maintainer/organisation channel when available. Do not place personal, security-sensitive or confidential details in a public issue merely to request help.

## Enforcement

Maintainers may edit or remove content, request changes, close conversations, restrict participation or take other proportionate moderation actions when behaviour conflicts with this standard.

Enforcement should focus on protecting participants and the quality of the project rather than winning technical disagreements.

## Good-faith correction

People can make mistakes. Prompt correction, acknowledgement of evidence and improved behaviour should be treated differently from repeated or deliberate abuse.

## Project principle

> Be demanding about evidence and architecture, and respectful toward the people doing the work.
