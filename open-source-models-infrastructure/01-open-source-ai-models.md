# 01 — Open Source AI Models

## Core Principle

Open source AI models are not automatically the best choice. They are another engineering option that must be evaluated against capability, cost, control, security, and business requirements.

## What Does Open Source Mean?

The AI ecosystem contains several different categories:

- Fully open source: code, weights, data and licences are available.
- Open weights: model parameters are available but training details may be limited.
- Source available: some components are visible but restrictions exist.
- Commercial hosted models: capability is accessed through an API.

These differences matter for enterprise adoption.

## When Open Models Make Sense

Open models can provide advantages:

- private deployment;
- data control;
- customisation;
- predictable infrastructure costs;
- specialised domain applications.

## When Hosted Models Make Sense

Frontier hosted models often provide:

- stronger general reasoning;
- faster innovation;
- less operational burden;
- multimodal capabilities;
- enterprise support.

## Enterprise Decision Framework

Do not ask:

> Which model is the best?

Ask:

> Which architecture creates the best business outcome?

Evaluate:

- accuracy;
- latency;
- cost;
- privacy;
- maintenance;
- licensing;
- scalability.

## Hybrid Future

## Relevance to AI Business Platforms

For systems like SEMLIS, different workloads may require different models:

- strategic reasoning → frontier model;
- classification → smaller specialised model;
- private analysis → controlled deployment;
- repetitive tasks → efficient local models.

The winning architecture will not be one model. It will be an intelligent model ecosystem.
