## Interpretation

Line count is a visibility signal, not a quality gate. The curriculum authoring standard prioritizes better architectural judgement over page count. A short module can be acceptable when its learning outcome, exercise, trade-offs and decision boundary are clear.

Coverage columns are lexical evidence that a domain contains architecture, failure/risk, security/governance and evaluation/testing discussion. They are not substitutes for technical review, and those topics only need dedicated treatment where relevant.

## Time-sensitive claims

P3 does not freeze model rankings, vendor prices or product leadership claims into the curriculum. The final gate distinguishes ordinary architecture statements from genuine current comparative claims. Current vendor/model comparisons that materially affect recommendations require nearby date/version/source evidence under the authoring standard.
