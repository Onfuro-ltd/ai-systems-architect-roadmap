# Competency Levels

The roadmap uses competency levels to prevent passive completion. Knowing vocabulary is useful, but it is not the same as being able to build or govern a production system.

## Level 1 — Understand

You can:
- explain the concept in plain and technical language;
- identify the problem it is intended to solve;
- describe its main components and trade-offs;
- distinguish it from adjacent concepts;
- recognise common marketing exaggerations.

Evidence may include a written explanation, architecture sketch or comparison.

## Level 2 — Build

You can:
- implement a representative working system;
- use the relevant APIs/protocols/tools;
- instrument the implementation sufficiently to inspect behaviour;
- reproduce common failure modes;
- test assumptions rather than relying on a demo.

Evidence should include runnable work, traces, tests or measured results.

## Level 3 — Architect

You can:
- decide when the technology should and should not be used;
- define system boundaries and interfaces;
- reason about reliability, security, cost, latency and scale;
- choose between competing patterns and providers;
- design fallbacks, observability and governance;
- separate replaceable implementation choices from durable architecture.

Evidence should include an architecture decision record, production design or comparative evaluation.

## Level 4 — Lead

You can:
- establish organisational standards and guardrails;
- design evaluation criteria for teams and vendors;
- communicate trade-offs to technical and non-technical stakeholders;
- govern risk and progressive autonomy;
- evolve architecture as models and standards change;
- build organisational knowledge that survives individual tools and providers.

Evidence should include standards, governance mechanisms, decision frameworks, post-incident learning or demonstrated stewardship of a production capability.

## Progression rule

Higher levels include the capabilities below them. A learner cannot credibly claim Architect-level mastery without practical exposure to implementation and failure modes.

The goal is not Level 4 in every topic. Depth should match responsibility and risk. A systems architect may require Level 3–4 in evaluation, security, orchestration and system design while maintaining Level 1–2 knowledge in specialised research topics.
