# 04 — Trust, Explainability and Provenance

## Purpose

Give users the evidence needed to evaluate important AI outputs without pretending hidden reasoning is a reliable explanation.

## Evidence

Show citations, source documents, timestamps, tool results, rules or structured factors when they materially support verification.

## Provenance

Track which source and system versions contributed to the result. Generated content should be distinguishable from authoritative facts.

## Explanation

Prefer concise user-facing reasons tied to observable evidence and rules.

Do not expose or depend on hidden chain-of-thought.

## Trust calibration

The goal is appropriate trust, not maximum trust. A product should make both capability and limitation discoverable.

## Freshness

When current data matters, communicate timestamp/freshness and allow re-checking.

## Audit vs UX

Detailed audit evidence and simple user explanations can be separate views built from the same structured provenance.

## Exercise

Design an explanation panel for an AI recommendation using three sources, one deterministic rule and one inferred conclusion.

## Takeaway

> Trustworthy AI products make important claims inspectable rather than asking users to trust the model's confidence or eloquence.
